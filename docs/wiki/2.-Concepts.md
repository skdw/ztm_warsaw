# 🧭 Concepts

## Stop ID and pole

| Term | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Stop ID** | the number shared by all poles of one stop | `7009` |
| **Pole number** | the pole (*słupek*) at that stop, two digits | `01`, `02` |

The stop ID is the street address and the pole is the flat. The number is printed on the pole's sign; a stop with several directions has a different pole for each.

## Scheduled, not live

The sensor shows **planned** departures from the City's timetable. It knows nothing about delays, diversions or cancelled trips. The City refreshes the timetable once a day and serves one day at a time: nothing for tomorrow. Where a vehicle is *can* be shown, see [The vehicle on the map](#the-vehicle-on-the-map).

## The service day and night lines

A City timetable is a **service day**, not a calendar day. Its times run from about 03:00 and carry on past midnight: a night bus at `26:20` in Friday's timetable runs at 02:20 on Saturday. The integration shows it at the right moment, and it does not disappear at 00:00.

Between about 03:00 and 05:00 the City already serves the next day, so the integration keeps the tail of the previous night while its departures are ahead.

## What the sensor shows

- **The state** is the time of the next departure. A card can display it as a countdown, and an automation can trigger on it.
- **After the last departure** the state is `unknown` and `Upcoming, Headsign` says `No service`. This is not an error: either the line does not run today, or its last bus has gone.
- **`unavailable`** means that the City answered without a timetable (it rejected the token, or it does not know the stop or line), or that the timetable the sensor holds is no longer valid. See [Troubleshooting](4.-Troubleshooting).

The attributes you will use most:

| Attribute | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `Upcoming, Headsign` | `Metro Bródno` | where the next departure goes |
| `Next 1, Departure Time` … `Next 3` | a time | the three after it |
| `Timetable, First Departure`, `Timetable, Last Departure` | `04:53`, `23:41` | the day's first and last departure, local time; they stay after the last bus has left |
| `Stop, Name`, `Stop, ID`, `Stop, Number` | `Rondo Daszyńskiego`, `1097`, `01` | the stop |
| `Line, Type` | `Tram line` | what kind of line it is |
| `Walking time, Minutes` | `6` | only when a walking time is set |

The picture of the sensor is a badge with the line number; on a map card it is the pin. Its colour follows ZTM's signs: ordinary lines light with a purple ring, fast red, zone green, night white on blue, express orange, tram light yellow, metro purple, urban rail green. The sensor has no position of its own: the stop pole is a pin of its own on the map (see below).

## The Status sensor

Every stop has a **Status** sensor that says in words what is going on. It stays available when the timetable is not, because explaining that is its job.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Running | the timetable is fine and departures are ahead |
| You cannot make it in time | every departure left is sooner than your walking time |
| The last departure of the day has gone | nothing more today |
| Does not run today | the line has no service today |
| Timetables are being updated | the City is replacing its data, about 02:10 to 03:05 |
| Waiting for the timetable | the first load has not finished |
| Could not get the timetable | the City did not answer properly |

## Stops and vehicles on the map

The integration puts two layers on the map. Each thing on the map has exactly one pin, however many lines and stops you set up.

- **Stop poles.** Every pole is one pin: a sign with the pole's number. If three of your lines stop at the pole, you still see one pin, and the lines are written under it (the pin's name is, for example, *Rondo Daszyńskiego 05 · 126, 176*, and the `Lines` attribute lists them). The pins appear on their own.
- **Vehicles.** A line shows its vehicles when you switch on *Show the vehicles of this line on the map* in **Configure** on the line (a token is needed). Each vehicle is one pin with the badge of its line, and its colour is the class of the line. The pin moves every 30 seconds and goes when the City stops reporting the vehicle or its last position is older than three minutes, so a parked vehicle is never drawn as if it were live. The attributes (the destination, the route, the operator and more) are listed in [Configuration](1.-Configuration#the-vehicles-of-a-line-on-the-map).

The pins are `geo_location` entities, so a map card lists them with one line, `geo_location_sources: [ztm_warsaw]` (see [Examples](3.-Examples)). A stop sensor has no position of its own, so a pole served by several lines is not drawn once for each.

### How far the bus is from the stop

When a line shows its vehicles (a token is needed), every stop of it also has a **Vehicle distance** sensor, on from the start and named after the stop; it goes when the vehicles are switched off. It is the distance in metres between the stop and the vehicle that will make the next departure, so an automation can start when the bus is 500 m away.

- **How it finds the vehicle.** Each departure in the timetable names its brigade, and the City reports where each vehicle of a line and brigade is. The sensor takes the vehicle of that brigade.
- **When it has no value.** Either the vehicle has not started the line yet, or the City does not report it, or its last position is older than three minutes.
- **The `Status` attribute** tells you why there is no value: `tracking` (there is one), `no_vehicle`, `old_position`, `too_early` (the next departure is more than 15 minutes away, so nothing is asked), `city_unavailable`, `no_brigade` (the timetable does not name the brigade of that departure) or `no_timetable`.
- **Load on the City.** All lines and stops that use the same token share one request every 30 seconds, and after a failure the integration waits longer between tries. The vehicle distance asks only while a next departure is near.
- **Not covered.** The City's vehicle data has no urban rail (S lines) and no metro, and it contains few vehicles at night.

## How and when it asks the City

- The timetable once an hour, plus once after the City has finished its overnight update. It does not ask between 02:10 and 03:05 and keeps the timetable it has.
- After a failed request it keeps the last timetable and asks again in five minutes, then less and less often; the log says it once.
- The stop's name comes from the City's catalogue of stops (1.6 MB): one download serves all your entries and is kept for a day.

## What the data cannot do

- A line that does not run today is not in the API, so it cannot be added that day.
- The journey planner on wtp.waw.pl has more data than the public API; a line that is there may be missing here.
- Until the API has the new day, the first departures of the morning (a tram that starts at 04:00, say) can be missing.
