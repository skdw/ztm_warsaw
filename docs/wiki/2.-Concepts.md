# 🧭 Concepts

## Stop ID and stop number

| Term | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Stop ID** | the number shared by all poles of one stop location | `7009` |
| **Stop number** | the pole (*słupek*) at that location, two digits | `01`, `02` |

The stop ID is the street address and the stop number is the flat. The number is printed on the pole's sign; a stop with several directions or platforms has a different number for each.

## Scheduled, not live

The sensor shows **planned** departures from the City's timetable. It knows nothing about delays, diversions or cancelled trips. The City refreshes the timetable once a day, and the API serves one day at a time: nothing for tomorrow, and no live data about delays. Where a vehicle is can be shown, see below.

## The vehicle on the map

With a token (not the old key) every stop has a **Vehicle** entity, switched off by default. Switch it on in the entity settings and it shows the vehicle that will make the next departure as a pin on the map, while that departure is less than 15 minutes away.

- **How it finds the vehicle:** a departure in the timetable names its brigade, and the City reports where every vehicle of a line and brigade is. The entity takes the vehicle of that brigade.
- **When there is no pin:** the vehicle has not started the line yet, the City does not report it, or its last position is older than three minutes. The City also keeps reporting vehicles that stand in a depot, and some at night, so an old position is never shown as if it were live. The `Status` attribute says why: `tracking`, `no_vehicle`, `old_position`, `too_early`, `city_unavailable` or `no_timetable`.
- **Attributes:** `Vehicle number`, `Brigade`, `Position time` and `Distance to stop` in metres, so an automation can start when the bus is 500 m away.
- **Load on the City:** all the stops that use the same token share one request every 30 seconds, and nothing is asked while no next departure is near. After a failure it waits longer between tries.
- **Trams and the urban rail:** trams are supported when the City reports them; the urban rail (S lines) and the metro are not in the City's vehicle data.

## The sensor

- **State:** the time of the next departure, as a timestamp. Cards can show it as a countdown and automations can trigger on it.
- **No departure left:** the state is `unknown` and the attribute `Upcoming, Headsign` says `No service`. The line does not run today, or its last departure has passed. The `Note` attribute says to check wtp.waw.pl or call 19115.
- **`unavailable`:** the City's API answered without a timetable (the key was rejected, the stop, pole and line are not known, an unknown format), or the timetable the sensor holds is no longer valid (its service day is over and nothing is left of the night); see [Troubleshooting](3.-Troubleshooting).
- **Status:** a *Status* sensor per stop says in plain words what is going on: *Running*, *You cannot make it in time* (every departure left is sooner than your walking time), *The last departure of the day has gone*, *Does not run today*, *Timetables are being updated* (about 02:10 to 03:05), *Waiting for the timetable* and *Could not get the timetable*. Unlike the main sensor it stays available when the timetable is not, because explaining that is its job.
- **Attributes:** the line (`Line, Number`, `Line, Type`, `Line, Full Timetable`), the stop (`Stop, ID`, `Stop, Number`, `Stop, Name`) and the next departure (`Upcoming, Headsign`, `Upcoming, Departure Day` as today, tomorrow, yesterday or a weekday, and more). The three departures after the next one are `Next 1, Departure Time`, `Next 2, ...` and `Next 3, ...`. The stop's position is in `latitude` and `longitude`, so it can be shown as a pin on the map card. The sensor's picture is a badge with the line number, which the map card draws as the pin instead of the initials of the name. Its colour is the class of the line, as on ZTM's own signs: ordinary lines (1xx, 2xx, 3xx) are light with a purple ring, fast ones (4xx, 5xx) red, zone lines green, night lines white on blue with a moon, express lines orange, and the others grey or teal. A dashed ring marks a periodic line (3xx, 4xx, 8xx, express). The shape is the vehicle: a circle for a bus, a rounded rectangle (light yellow) for a tram, a square for the metro (purple) and the urban rail (green). The attribution names the City of Warsaw and the licence. The same departures are available as events of the optional calendar entity (see [Configuration](1.-Configuration)). `Timetable, First Departure` and `Timetable, Last Departure` hold the first and the last departure of the day as local times: they stay when the state is *unknown* after the last departure, so a card can still say when the last bus left.

## The service day and night lines

A City timetable is a **service day**, not a calendar day. Its times run from about 03:00 and carry on past midnight: a night bus at `26:20` in the timetable of Friday runs at 02:20 on Saturday. The integration reads these times correctly, so a departure after midnight is shown at the right moment and does not disappear at 00:00.

Night lines (`N01`, `N44`, ...) run in these late hours. Between about 03:00 and 05:00 the City already serves the next day's timetable, so the integration keeps the last tail of the previous night for as long as its departures are ahead.

## How and when it asks the City

- Once an hour, plus once after the City has finished its overnight update. The City starts replacing its timetables at about 02:10, so the integration does not ask between 02:10 and 03:05 and keeps the timetable it has.
- A timeout or a server error after a good load keeps the last timetable (planned data does not change within a day); a rejected key does not, see [Troubleshooting](3.-Troubleshooting).
- The stop's name comes from the City's stop catalogue (1.6 MB). One download serves all your entries of the same kind of key and is kept in memory for a day (a restart of Home Assistant downloads it again).

## What the data cannot do

- Lines that do not run today are not in the API, so they cannot be added that day.
- The journey planner on wtp.waw.pl has more data than the public API; a line that is there may be missing here.
- The two City APIs may publish the new day at different times. Until the API you use has the new day, the sensor knows nothing about the first departures of the morning (a tram that starts at 04:00, say), so they can be missing.
